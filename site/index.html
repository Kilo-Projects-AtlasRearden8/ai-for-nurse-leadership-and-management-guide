What AI is (in plain English)

AI is software that can read, write, summarize, and organize information by recognizing patterns in huge amounts of text. It works a lot like a very fast, very well-read assistant:

  • It can draft emails, agendas, and policy language.
  • It can summarize long documents into bullet points.
  • It can help you brainstorm ideas and organize messy notes.
  • It can format tables, rewrite text for clarity, and translate between languages.

It is not a person. It does not have clinical judgment, a nursing license, or access to your patients' charts unless you give it information. It can be confidently wrong — this is called "hallucinating." Always read and verify anything AI produces before you use it.

The golden rule for healthcare

Never paste patient names, birth dates, medical record numbers, diagnoses, or any other protected health information (PHI) into a public AI tool. Treat AI like a public whiteboard: assume anything you type could be read by others.

Use de-identified, made-up examples only. If you need to work with real staffing or patient data, keep it inside systems your organization has already approved and vetted for AI use.

How to use each section

  1. Read the section straight through once.
  2. Follow the step-by-step instructions.
  3. Fill in the printable worksheet at the end of each section (or the combined worksheets page).
  4. Adapt everything to your unit, your hospital's policies, and your state's nursing regulations.
