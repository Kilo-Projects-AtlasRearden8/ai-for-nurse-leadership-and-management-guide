# Policy Drafts: AI Use Policy for Your Nursing Unit

An AI use policy tells staff what is and isn't okay when using AI at work. This section gives you a fill-in-the-blank policy template and the steps to adopt it on your unit.

**Reminder: This is a template for discussion, not legal advice. Have your organization's legal, compliance, HR, and nursing leadership review any policy before adoption.**

---

## Why your unit needs an AI policy

Staff are already using AI — sometimes without telling you. A written policy does three things:

1. Sets expectations (what's allowed, what's not).
2. Protects patients (keeps PHI out of AI tools).
3. Protects you (gives you a clear basis for coaching and corrections).

You don't need a 20-page document. Two pages of clear rules is enough to start.

---

## What belongs in a unit AI policy

| Section | What to cover |
|---------|---------------|
| Purpose | Why the policy exists and who it applies to |
| Allowed uses | Drafting emails, agendas, handouts, brainstorming (de-identified only) |
| Prohibited uses | Patient information, staff personal information, clinical decisions, charting, personnel decisions |
| Privacy rule | No PHI in public AI tools; follow HIPAA and organization policy |
| Human review | A human reads and approves everything before use |
| Reporting | Who to tell if AI is misused or gives wrong information |
| Review date | When the policy will be reviewed again (recommend quarterly) |

## Sample policy language (template)

Fill in the bracketed sections. Remove anything that doesn't fit your unit.

```
AI USE POLICY — [UNIT NAME]
============================
Effective date: __________      Review date: __________
Approved by: __________________

1. Purpose
This policy describes how staff on [unit name] may use artificial intelligence (AI)
tools for work-related tasks. It is an educational support tool, not a clinical
decision-making tool.

2. Allowed uses
Staff may use AI to draft, summarize, or reorganize non-sensitive work materials,
including:
   - Meeting agendas and minutes
   - Staff announcements and newsletters
   - Staff recognition and appreciation ideas
   - Drafting unit policies and procedures for leadership review
   - Brainstorming process-improvement ideas

All materials must be de-identified: no patient or staff identifiers.

3. Prohibited uses
Staff must NOT use AI for:
   - Entering patient names, birth dates, MRNs, diagnoses, or any protected
     health information (PHI)
   - Entering staff names, contact information, or other personal data
   - Writing or completing patient chart entries
   - Making clinical assessments, diagnoses, or care directions
   - Making hiring, discipline, evaluation, or scheduling decisions about staff
   - Replacing clinical judgment or professional nursing judgment

4. Privacy and security
All AI use must follow [organization name] privacy, HIPAA, and information-security
policies. Assume anything typed into a public AI tool may be retained by the tool's
provider. When in doubt, do not enter the information.

5. Human review
A human supervisor must read and approve any AI-generated material before it is
shared, posted, or placed in a patient record or personnel file. AI can produce
incorrect information. The person who uses the output is responsible for its
accuracy.

6. Reporting problems
Report any misuse of AI, any accidental entry of patient or staff information, or
any incorrect AI output used in patient care to:
   [nurse manager name / educator / compliance contact]
   [contact method]

7. Review
This policy will be reviewed every [quarter / six months] by [nurse manager] and
updated as needed.

Acknowledgment: I have read and understand this policy.
Signature: ____________________    Date: __________
```

---

## Adoption steps

**Step 1: Draft the policy.** Use the template above. Fill in your unit name, dates, and contacts.

**Step 2: Get reviewers.** Send the draft to:
- Your nurse director or administrator
- The compliance or privacy officer
- HR (if the policy touches staff conduct)
- Your unit educator or clinical practice council

**Step 3: Revise.** Incorporate feedback. Keep it short — one to two pages.

**Step 4: Educate staff.** Review the policy at a staff huddle. Hand out copies. Post it in the break room and on the unit bulletin board.

**Step 5: Acknowledge.** Ask each staff member to sign an acknowledgment (see the bottom of the template).

**Step 6: Review on a schedule.** Set a calendar reminder to revisit the policy every quarter. AI tools change fast; your policy should too.
