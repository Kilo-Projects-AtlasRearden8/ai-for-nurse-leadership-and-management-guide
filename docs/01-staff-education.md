# Staff Education: Teaching Your Team About AI

Your staff are already hearing about AI — from the news, from friends, and possibly from patients. As a nurse manager, you don't need to be an expert. You need to give your team a safe, simple starting point.

This section walks you through a 30-minute staff huddle on AI, plus ground rules, common questions, and a printable worksheet.

**Reminder: This is education only. Do not use AI outputs to make clinical assignments, staffing decisions, or personnel decisions without review.**

---

## Before the huddle: prepare yourself

**Step 1: Pick one AI tool to demonstrate.** Choose a free, widely available tool (for example, a public chatbot). Open it on a laptop or tablet you can project. Do not log in with personal or work accounts that contain sensitive data if you can avoid it.

**Step 2: Choose one safe demonstration topic.** Good examples:
- Drafting a shift-change reminder for staff
- Rewriting a unit announcement for clarity
- Brainstorming recognition ideas for Nurses Week

Bad examples: anything with real patient names, real staff names, or real schedules.

**Step 3: Know your ground rules.** Write these on a whiteboard or handout before you start:
1. No patient names, birth dates, MRNs, or any protected health information.
2. No staff names or personal information.
3. AI is a drafting assistant, not a decision-maker. A human reviews everything.
4. If AI says something about medication, dosing, or a clinical condition, treat it as suspect until a clinical expert confirms it.

**Step 4: Schedule 30 minutes.** A huddle at shift change, a unit council meeting, or a lunch-and-learn all work.

## The 30-minute AI huddle

| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 0–5 min | Welcome and why we're talking about AI |
| 5–12 min | Live demo: one tool, one safe task |
| 12–20 min | Ground rules and privacy reminders |
| 20–27 min | Staff brainstorm: tasks AI could help with |
| 27–30 min | Next steps and where to bring questions |

### Minute 0–5: Welcome

Say something like:

> "AI is showing up everywhere in healthcare, and you might already be using it or hearing about it. Today we're going to spend 30 minutes on what AI is, how we *can* use it on this unit, and — just as importantly — how we *won't* use it. Nothing we do today changes how you care for patients."

### Minute 5–12: Live demo

Show one safe task. Example script:

> "I'm going to ask AI to rewrite this unit announcement so it's shorter and friendlier. Watch what it produces — and watch me read it carefully before I send it. AI can miss the tone we need on a busy unit."

After the demo, ask: "What did you notice? What would you change?"

### Minute 12–20: Ground rules

Review the four ground rules above. Emphasize:

- **PHI:** If it identifies a patient, it never goes into AI.
- **Verify:** AI can sound confident and be wrong. A nurse reads everything.
- **No clinical direction:** AI does not assess patients or direct care.

### Minute 20–27: Staff brainstorm

Ask staff: "What's a repetitive writing or organizing task on this unit that AI might help with?" Write ideas on a whiteboard. Common answers:
- Drafting staff meeting agendas
- Rewriting patient education handouts for simpler reading levels
- Creating shift reminder lists
- Brainstorming staff recognition ideas

Capture ideas on the **Staff AI Education Worksheet** at the end of this section.

### Minute 27–30: Next steps

- Pick one idea from the brainstorm to try next week.
- Assign one person to bring results back to the next huddle.
- Post the ground rules where staff can see them.
- Tell staff who to ask if they're unsure whether a use is appropriate (you, the educator, or the compliance contact).

## Common staff questions — and plain-English answers

**"Will AI replace my job?"**
No. AI is a tool, like an electronic health record or a phone. It cannot provide nursing care, build relationships with patients, or use clinical judgment. Your judgment is the important part.

**"Is AI private?"**
Not by default. Assume anything you type into a public AI tool could be saved or reviewed by the company that runs it. Never put patient or staff information in.

**"Can AI write my charting for me?"**
No — and you should not let it. Charting must reflect your own assessment and care. Copying AI-generated notes into a chart is a documentation integrity problem.

**"Can AI make the assignment sheet?"**
AI can help you *draft* ideas, but you — using your knowledge of your staff, your patients, and your unit — make the final assignment decisions. AI does not know your unit the way you do.

**"What if AI gives wrong medical information?"**
It can. That's why every AI output about health or care gets checked by a clinical expert before anyone uses it.

## Ground rules handout (copy and post)

```
AI GROUND RULES — THIS UNIT
============================
1. No patient names, birth dates, MRNs, or any protected health information.
2. No staff names or personal information.
3. AI drafts, humans decide. Read everything before you use it.
4. AI never directs patient care or makes clinical judgments.
5. AI never makes hiring, discipline, or scheduling decisions.
6. Unsure? Ask the nurse manager or educator before you use it.
```
